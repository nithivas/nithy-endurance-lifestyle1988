My endurance story
From First Lap to Ironman Finish Line

Running found me in 2013. What started as a way to stay fit became a lifestyle — half marathons, road races, then the pull of something bigger. In 2023 I added swimming and cycling, stepping into the world of triathlon. What started as a curiosity — could I actually swim, bike, and run back to back — became an obsession that reshaped everything about how I train, eat, and think.

"Ironman is 95% mental. The body will do what the mind demands of it — but you have to train the mind first, one early morning at a time."

The Ironman journey is unlike anything in endurance sport. A 1.9 km open water swim, a 90 km bike leg at race pace, and a 21 km run — each discipline hard enough alone, devastating when chained together. Racing Ironman 70.3 Desaru Coast taught me what I was made of. The cold rain on the bike, the burning legs at kilometre 15 of the run — and then the finish line. Arms up. Done.

Swimming is the newest discipline and the one that has grown fastest. The very first pool session was December 2022 — 572 metres, just getting comfortable with the water. Structured training began in earnest in 2023, building from 400 metre sessions to holding 2:20/100m pool pace and completing a 2.54 km open water swim by 2026. OW pace improved from 3:48/100m in May 2023 to 2:44/100m in June 2026 — three years of drill-focused pool sessions and weekly open water practice.

Cycling also started in 2023 and transformed fast with structured training. From 18.8 km/h average in those first sessions to consistently pushing 27–31 km/h on long rides by 2026. Century rides are now a regular training block, and the OCBC Cycle series has become a fixture — completing 800 km in the inter-division ride alone. The power numbers keep climbing toward IRONMAN race pace.

Running is where it all began — back in 2013. Over 13 years of pre-dawn runs, night races, and track sessions have brought the half marathon from 2:02:27 in 2017 to 1:54:42 in April 2026. The biomechanics data shows ground contact time at an all-time low, cadence trending toward 170 spm — the body is becoming more efficient with every race.

Endurance Squad is not just a tri club — it is the community that makes early mornings possible. Training partners who push you when your legs say stop. Teammates who celebrate every finish line as if it were their own. Starting triathlon in 2023 and targeting a full IRONMAN in Langkawi by November 2026 — that is three years from first swim lesson to the finish line of the world's toughest one-day endurance race.

"Every long ride, every open water session, every track repeat is a deposit in an account you draw from on race day. The bank never lies."
