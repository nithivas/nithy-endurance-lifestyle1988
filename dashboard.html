What your Garmin data specifically tells us to fix
📈
Swolf plateauing at ~99
Your swolf has increased from 91.9 (2023) to 99.8 (2025) — this means your stroke is adding more strokes without saving time. Classic sign of overreaching distance before mastering technique. Solution: Short sharp drill sets before every main set.
🌊
OW gap of ~33s/100m
Pool avg 2:23 vs OW avg 2:56 — a 33s/100m gap. Industry standard for trained triathletes is <15s. Your OW sighting every 10+ strokes costs 5–8s/100m alone. Adding bilateral breathing and mass-start practice can close 15s of this gap within 6 weeks.
⚡
No structured speedwork
Your sessions are mostly continuous swims without interval structure. Your best pace (1:47–2:04/100m) shows speed potential. Adding 1× interval session weekly — 10×100m with short rest — will drop your CSS pace by 8–12s/100m in 8 weeks.
💓
HR jumped 2023→2024
Pool avg HR went from 96 bpm (2023) to 137 bpm (2024) and stayed there. This suggests your base volume was low early on. Now your aerobic base is solid — it is time to add polarised training: 80% easy (120–130bpm) + 20% hard (>155bpm).
🔄
Stroke rate 24 spm
Your avg stroke rate of 24 strokes/min is on the lower side for triathlon racing. Elite age-groupers hit 28–32 spm. Using a tempo trainer beeping at 28 spm for 2 sessions/week will build the neuromuscular pattern without losing distance-per-stroke.
📅
Consistency gaps
Several months with only 4–6 sessions. Swimming rewards consistency more than any other triathlon discipline. 3× sessions every week — even short 1,000m sessions — beats 1× mega-session. Your 2026 trend (8 sessions/month avg) is the right direction.

What your Garmin biomechanics data tells us to fix
🦵
Cadence: 157 spm — needs to reach 170+
Your cadence dropped from 163 spm (2017) to 148 spm (2023–24) and is recovering to 157 in 2026. The optimal range for your pace is 170–175 spm. Each 5 spm increase reduces injury risk ~14% and improves economy. Use a metronome app or Garmin's run cadence alert set to 170.
⏱️
GCT: 272ms — target 250ms
Ground contact time of 272ms is improving (was 283ms in 2023–24) but still above the <260ms target for sub-1:50 half pace. Drills that help: A-skips, bounding, and barefoot strides on grass 2×/week. Each 10ms reduction in GCT yields ~3–4 seconds per km.
📈
Vertical oscillation: 10.3cm — target 9.5cm
Your vert osc is best ever at 10.3cm in 2026. Reducing to 9.5cm would save approximately 8–10 seconds per km by wasting less energy moving up and down. Focus: lean slightly forward from ankles (not waist), drive elbows back not up.
🔥
No structured intervals until 2024
Your track sessions only started in 2024 and your pace jumped immediately — 5:30/km halves in 2024, 4:43/km track pace by 2026. This is the biggest untapped lever. Adding a second quality session per week (tempo + track) can realistically shave 6–8 min off your half in one cycle.
❤️
HR 159–167 on easy runs — too high
Your easy run HR averages 159–167 bpm — well above the aerobic zone (130–145 for your likely max of ~185). You're running too hard on easy days, which limits recovery and aerobic adaptation. Slow down easy runs by 45–60 sec/km — it feels too slow but builds a bigger base.
📅
Volume inconsistency 2018–2023
Big training gaps: 30 sessions in 2018, 11 in 2019, 4 in 2022. Your 2024–2025 consistency (80–92 sessions/year) is where gains happened. Maintaining 4 runs/week minimum throughout the year — even easy 5 km runs — is worth more than big training blocks followed by gaps.

📉

Your average NP fell from 150W (2024) to 123W (2025). This coincides with the Speedmax TT bike introduction and longer rides — you traded intensity for volume. In 2026 NP is recovering to 129W. A structured FTP block will reclaim and surpass your 2024 peak power.

Your cadence improved dramatically from 56 rpm (2023) to 81 rpm (2026) but still short of the optimal 85–95 rpm for triathlon. Low cadence forces higher muscular stress, leaving your legs more fatigued for the run. Indoor erg sessions with a cadence target of 90 rpm will build the neuromuscular pattern quickly.
⚖️
L/R imbalance: 46% L / 54% R

🧱
No structured Z2 base before 2024
Only 2 sessions before 2023. Your aerobic cycling base is still relatively young despite the speed improvements. Elite cyclists spend 80% of training in Zone 2 (55–75% FTP). Adding 2× 90-min Z2 rides per week will build mitochondrial density and make threshold work more effective.
🏁
